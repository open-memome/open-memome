# Methodology

## Objective

Open Memome maps culturally transmitted units, their variants, documented occurrences, propagation measurements, and relationships. Semantic proximity suggests similarity. It does not establish ancestry, causation, truth, benefit, or harm.

## Working definitions

**Meme:** a learned cultural unit that recurs in recognizable form and can vary while remaining related.

**Memome:** the set of memes present in a population, together with their variants and relationships.

These are operational definitions. “Meme” and “memome” remain contested terms. Cultural-evolution research often studies cultural traits without assuming gene-like, high-fidelity replication.

## Five-gate test

A candidate must be:

1. **Transmitted.** Learned from another person, group, artifact, or system.
2. **Recognizable.** Expressible as one neutral, copyable unit.
3. **Recurrent.** Present in at least two independent occurrences.
4. **Variable.** Capable of variation without losing family resemblance.
5. **Traceable.** Located through dated evidence of movement or persistence.

A person, product, organization, event, place, or broad subject is normally a carrier or context. Popularity is measured separately from meme fit.

## Research basis

The test synthesizes several traditions:

- [Richard Dawkins](https://global.oup.com/academic/product/the-selfish-gene-9780198788607) introduced the meme as a unit of cultural transmission in *The Selfish Gene* (1976).
- [O'Brien, Lyman, Mesoudi, and VanPool](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2981907/) examine how cultural traits can serve as identifiable units of transmission while warning that units require analytical clarity.
- [Alex Mesoudi](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/C/bo8787504.html) describes cultural evolution through variation, inheritance, competition, and multiple empirical methods.
- [Limor Shifman](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262317702/memes-in-digital-culture/) treats internet memes as related groups of items that are circulated, imitated, and transformed.
- [The Cultural Evolution Society](https://culturalevolutionsociety.org/about-cultural-evolution/what-is-cultural-evolution/) summarizes the similarities and important differences between genetic and cultural inheritance.

The five gates belong to Open Memome. They are a review protocol built from this literature, not a quotation or universal standard.

## Evidence

A documented record needs at least two relevant, independent, dated occurrences. Each source must support the specific claim attached to it. A reference page that merely names a topic may support discovery, but not recurrence or lineage.

Source preference:

1. Primary evidence or a stable archive of the occurrence
2. Peer-reviewed research or an authoritative scholarly reference
3. High-quality secondary reporting
4. Community archives with transparent provenance

Claims about origin, influence, opposition, or effects require their own evidence. Absence of evidence remains visible.

## Record states

- **Discovery lead:** imported catalogue match; meme fit unassessed
- **Candidate:** copyable unit defined; evidence or scope incomplete
- **Documented:** at least two relevant dated sources attached to a scoped unit
- **Contested:** substantive disagreement remains unresolved
- **Rejected:** outside scope or unsupported
- **Redirected:** duplicate or better represented as another record type

Discovery leads are excluded from the default public map.

## Reach and virality

Reach, current attention, propagation velocity, persistence, fidelity, and mutation rate are different measurements. Every observation should state its source, date range, geography, channel, host population, and denominator.

The current bubble-size proxy is Wikimedia sitelink count. It is reproducible and cross-lingual, but biased toward encyclopedic coverage. It does not measure current attention or diffusion speed. The interface labels it as long-run footprint and leaves missing values unscored.

A time slider will require comparable dated propagation observations. Founding dates will not be used as a substitute for historical influence.

## Semantic map

The layout uses record names, definitions, types, and collections. Nearby points share language and meaning. Cluster labels summarize computed neighbourhoods and remain open to review.

Origin, influence, reinforcement, opposition, and co-occurrence must be stored as separately sourced relationships. Distance alone never proves them.

## Review principles

- Review one claim at a time.
- Prefer independent evidence over repeated citation chains.
- Preserve credible minority rationales.
- Declare conflicts of interest.
- Record uncertainty rather than forcing agreement.
- Keep all accepted changes and reversals in version history.

The method may change through public proposals. Material changes require a decision record in `docs/decisions/`.
